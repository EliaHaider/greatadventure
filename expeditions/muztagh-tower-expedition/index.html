About this expedition

Muztagh Tower (7,273 m) is a steep spire of rock and ice rising above the Baltoro. Teams walk from Askole through Paiju and the lower Baltoro, then establish base camp for the chosen side of the mountain, commonly near the Muztagh or Younghusband glacier systems.

We handle this peak as a specialist private expedition. Permits, porter loads, base camp, food, transport and communications are planned around your exact route and climbing style. Our standard programme is 45 days, and it can be adapted to your team and objective.

Who this expedition is for

Elite technical teams with sustained alpine rock, mixed and ice climbing, difficult route-finding, multi-day bivouacs, complex descents, crevasse rescue and previous high-altitude expedition experience. Climbing is not guided: your team leads the climbing, and our staff run the camp, kitchen and support behind you.

Route notes

  • Askole to Paiju and onto the Baltoro Glacier, with the Trango towers coming into view on the way.
  • A secluded base camp beneath Muztagh Tower, with access to the Younghusband and neighbouring glaciers and close views of the Baltoro Muztagh.
  • The glacier approach is moraine and active ice; the mountain routes can combine icefall, steep snow and ice, and long rock and mixed ridges.
  • The route and glacier must be confirmed before permits, porter loads and the base camp position are final.
  • Hotels in Islamabad and Skardu. Expedition tents, a dining and communications tent and cooked meals at base camp.

Peaks and history

1956 — First ascent. Two rival expeditions, one British and one French, climbed the twin-summited spire within days of each other, from opposite sides of the mountain.

Common questions

Which glacier is used for base camp?

It depends on the route you choose. The route and glacier approach are confirmed first, and only then are the permits, porter loads and base camp position finalised.

Is Muztagh Tower a guided commercial climb?

No. We do not offer a standard guided ascent. Any guiding or high-altitude support needs a specialist review of the climbers, the route, staffing and the limits of rescue.

Is there a summit guarantee?

No. Weather, snow, ice, rockfall, avalanche exposure, team condition and route feasibility decide what is possible, and the expedition leader may turn the team around at any point.

Is helicopter evacuation guaranteed?

No. A helicopter depends on weather, daylight, aircraft availability, authorisation, insurance approval and a usable extraction site. Every team needs a ground evacuation plan.

What are the main hazards?

Loose rock, serac and avalanche exposure, rapid warming, storm loading, limited bivouac sites and a complicated descent. Conservative timing and a stable forecast are essential.

When is the best time to go?

Summer. The climbing window is usually June to August.

Can the support package be customised?

Yes. Base-camp-only support, high-altitude porters, oxygen, communications and private-team arrangements can be scoped once we have seen the route, climbing resumes, dates and team size.

Is the listed price fixed?

The listed price is a starting price per person. The final price depends on your group size and the number of days, and we confirm it with you in a written proposal after we discuss your plan.
