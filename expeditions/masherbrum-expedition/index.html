About this expedition

Masherbrum, also known as K1, rises to 7,821 metres above the Hushe valley in the Karakoram. It is one of Pakistan's most serious high mountains and should be approached as a major technical expedition, not a commercial trekking objective.

The standard access runs from Islamabad to Skardu, then through Khaplu to Hushe. From Hushe the team walks to Masherbrum Base Camp and sets up a self-contained climbing operation. The climb itself needs expert route judgement, glacier travel, steep snow and ice, high camps and conservative weather decisions.

Who this expedition is for

Highly experienced alpine climbers with a record of technical high-altitude climbing, glacier navigation and independent expedition decision-making. Members should be comfortable with difficult route finding, long waits and self-managed camps, and accept that conditions may prevent a summit attempt.

Route notes

  • Remote Hushe valley approach, with Skardu-based cargo and porter logistics.
  • Designed for small, highly experienced teams.
  • Far less traffic than K2 or Broad Peak, with a strategy shaped by route condition and weather.

Mountain history

1960 — First ascent. Masherbrum was first climbed in 1960 by an American-Pakistani expedition.

Historic name — K1. Masherbrum was originally given the survey designation K1, before K2 became the better-known peak.

Common questions

Is Masherbrum the same as K1?

Yes. It was historically designated K1 and rises above the Hushe valley.

How technical is it?

It is a serious technical high-altitude mountain, suitable only for climbers with strong alpine, glacier, snow, ice and expedition skills.

Where does the approach start?

By road from Skardu through Khaplu to Hushe, then on foot to Masherbrum Base Camp.

Why is the group size small?

The mountain is remote, technical and complex. Small teams are easier to manage safely and allow more disciplined decisions.

Why is there no fixed price online?

The fair price depends on group size, dates, porter allowance, oxygen choice and the level of high-altitude support. We prefer to understand your team first and then send a clear written proposal.

Are permits included?

Yes. We handle the mountaineering permit, peak royalty, environment fees, liaison officer coordination and the official briefing and debriefing. We need your passport copies, visa details and insurance documents well before arrival.

What's Included

  • Domestic flight (Islamabad – Skardu – Islamabad, weather permitting), with road transfer support if flights are cancelled
  • Invitation letter for your Pakistan visa application
  • Islamabad airport pickup and drop-off
  • Expedition permit, peak royalty, liaison officer coordination and environment fees
  • Hotel stays in Islamabad and Skardu, twin share
  • 4x4 transport from Skardu to Hushe via Khaplu, and back
  • Porters for expedition loads from Hushe to base camp and back, within the agreed allowance
  • All meals on the approach, at base camp and during the climbing period
  • Base camp tents (sleeping, kitchen, dining, toilet and shower) and satellite communication at base camp
  • Cook, kitchen crew, base camp staff and local high-altitude support staff
  • Staff wages, food, equipment and insurance

What's Not Included

  • International flights, Pakistan visa fee and airport taxes
  • Personal travel, medical and rescue insurance (mountaineering and helicopter evacuation cover is mandatory)
  • Personal climbing clothing and equipment
  • Supplemental oxygen, regulator and mask (available on request), and a personal high-altitude porter or private climbing guide, unless added to the written proposal
  • Personal satellite phone, internet and extra battery charging
  • Extra hotel nights, meals and transport caused by weather, flight cancellations or road closures
  • Rescue, helicopter evacuation, hospital and repatriation costs
  • Tips and summit bonuses for guides, porters and kitchen staff
  • Anything not listed under What's Included or in your written proposal
