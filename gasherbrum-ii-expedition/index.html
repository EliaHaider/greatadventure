About this expedition

Gasherbrum II, 8,035 m, is the thirteenth-highest mountain in the world and the second-highest summit of the Gasherbrum group. It is often described as one of the more approachable 8,000 m peaks, but that can mislead: the mountain is remote, cold, crevassed and fully exposed to Karakoram weather.

For strong climbers it is a sensible first 8,000 m objective after serious 6,000 m and 7,000 m experience. The usual plan follows the Gasherbrum base camp approach from Concordia, then uses three high camps above base camp with rotations before the summit push. July and early August normally give the best mix of access, snow and weather.

Who this expedition is for

Strong climbers who want a serious but logical step into 8,000 m expedition climbing. Best with previous 6,000 m or 7,000 m climbs, good cold-camping habits and the patience to wait for weather. It is not for trekkers who simply want to try altitude.

Route notes

  • Baltoro and Concordia approach into the Gasherbrum basin.
  • Remote base camp below the Gasherbrum group, at around 5,000 m.
  • A staged high-camp climb where weather, snow condition and acclimatisation control the schedule.

Mountain history

7 July 1956 — First ascent. The Austrian trio of Fritz Moravec, Josef Larch and Hans Willenpart made the first ascent via the southwest and east ridges, which form the basis of today's normal route.

2 February 2011 — First winter ascent. Simone Moro, Denis Urubko and Cory Richards made the first winter ascent, also the first winter ascent of any 8,000 m peak in the Karakoram.

Common questions

Is Gasherbrum II a good first 8,000 m peak?

For the right climber, yes, but only after strong altitude experience. You should already know how your body behaves above 6,000 m and be confident with crampons, fixed ropes and glacier travel.

When is the best season?

Summer, especially July into early August. June can still hold unsettled snow and late August gets less certain.

How many high camps are used?

Most teams use three above base camp. The number of rotations and the timing of the final push depend on acclimatisation, snow, rope-fixing progress and the weather window.

Why is there no fixed price online?

The fair price depends on group size, dates, porter allowance, oxygen choice and the level of high-altitude support. We prefer to understand your team first and then send a clear written proposal.

Are permits included?

Yes. We handle the mountaineering permit, peak royalty, environment fees, liaison officer coordination and the official briefing and debriefing. We need your passport copies, visa details and insurance documents well before arrival.
