About the Snow Lake & Hispar La trek

This 23-day trek joins two of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions. From Askole it follows the 62 km Biafo Glacier up to Snow Lake (Lukpe Lawo), a huge high snowfield ringed by peaks of 6,000 m and more, crosses the Hispar La at 5,151 m, then descends the 53 km Hispar Glacier to Hispar village in the Nagar valley.

Once you leave Baintha, the last grassy camp, there is no village, no road and no shelter until Hispar, so the team carries everything it needs. Great Adventure runs the traverse from Skardu with the guides, cooks, porters and drivers organised directly for your group, and a vehicle waits at Hispar for the drive on to Gilgit.

Who this trek is for

Experienced trekkers who have already done high routes such as K2 Base Camp or Gondogoro La, or comparable Himalayan treks, and want a long, remote glacier journey. Plan on 6 to 8 hours of walking a day with a daypack. This is not a casual sightseeing trek: the glacier surface is broken ice and moraine, and help is always days away.

Route notes

  • Askole to Hispar village on foot: roughly 115 km of glacier travel across moraine, ice and snow, with no maintained path.
  • Several nights above 4,500 m, with the highest camp at about 5,000 m below the Hispar La.
  • The Hispar La headwall is steep snow and ice (about 45 to 50 degrees). Crampons and an ice axe are needed, and the guides fix ropes on the steepest section.
  • Route-finding changes each season, especially around Snow Lake and the Hispar La, and the crossing is made only when snow and weather allow.
  • Hotels in Islamabad, Skardu and Gilgit. Tented camps with a mess tent, cooked meals and boiled water on the trek.

About Snow Lake and the Hispar La

Snow Lake (Lukpe Lawo) — A white plateau in the sky. Snow Lake sits at about 4,800 m, one of the largest high ice fields outside the poles. The silence and scale are unlike anything else on a Karakoram trek. The sun is intense at this height, so full sun protection matters all day.

The crossing — The hardest day. The day over the Hispar La starts at 4:00 to 5:00 AM so the team reaches the pass in stable conditions. After the headwall comes a steep descent onto the Hispar Glacier. Expect 7 to 9 hours in total.

The Hispar side — Giants and long ice. The upper Hispar Glacier is wide and crevassed, under peaks such as Pumari Chhish (7,492 m) and Kanjut Sar (7,760 m). The ice slowly turns to moraine and scrub before the village of Hispar.

Best time — Mid-July to mid-August. Before late June the Hispar La can carry serious avalanche risk. By September fresh snow builds up and nights at Snow Lake turn very cold, so we run departures only in this window.

Common questions

What fitness level do I need?

You should be able to walk 6 to 8 hours a day for up to 17 days with a daypack. Previous trekking above 4,000 m within the last year is strongly advised, and you should have used crampons on a glacier before. Start training 3 to 4 months before departure.

When is the best time to go?

Mid-July to mid-August. Earlier in the season the Hispar La can carry serious avalanche risk, and by September fresh snow and cold nights make the crossing harder. All departures are planned within this window.

How technical is the Hispar La?

The final headwall is about 45 to 50 degrees of snow and ice and needs crampons and an ice axe, with fixed ropes on the steepest part. It is not technical mountaineering, but it is far more than a hiking pass, so you must be comfortable on steep snow.

How many porters will there be?

For a group of 6 to 8 trekkers, expect roughly 22 to 28 porters for the full traverse, plus the cook and kitchen staff. Loads are capped at 25 kg, and porters are insured and properly equipped.

What if someone gets altitude sickness?

Guides carry altitude medication, emergency oxygen and a satellite communicator. Anyone with severe symptoms must descend at once. A helicopter evacuation from the glacier depends on weather and can take many hours to arrange, so travel insurance with helicopter evacuation cover is mandatory.

Can the trek be done in the other direction?

Yes. It can start at Hispar village, climb the Hispar Glacier, cross the Hispar La and come down the Biafo to Askole and Skardu. Contact us to discuss it.

Which permits are needed?

The Biafo–Hispar trekking area permit and the No Objection Certificate are arranged by us, together with any other access permit the route needs. We need passport copies at least 3 weeks before departure, and processing usually takes 5 to 7 working days.

Why is there no fixed price online?

The fair price depends on group size, dates, number of porters and the support you want. We prefer to learn about your team first and then send a clear written proposal.
